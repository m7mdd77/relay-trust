# Two-year thesis: trust should travel with the task

By 2028, useful agent identity will combine stable accountability with temporary authority. A public key can prove which actor signed a message. It cannot prove that the actor deserves access, understands the task, or still represents its owner.

The next trust layer will therefore separate three questions: who is speaking, what evidence supports their competence, and what exactly may they do now? Reputation should be a collection of attributable, context-specific receipts and attestations. A universal score hides who vouched, what was measured, and whether yesterday's success matters to today's risk.

Delegation should be explicit, narrow, time-limited, recipient-bound, and revocable. Receivers should evaluate it at the action boundary. A task approved before a grant is withdrawn must not execute afterward. Repeated requests should return the original result instead of multiplying effects.

Interoperable credentials will matter, but syntax alone cannot create trust. Systems still need authenticated issuers, key rotation, compromise recovery, privacy-preserving evidence sharing, and policies that account for conflicting attestations. Operators will need to distinguish a cryptographically authentic claim from a credible claim.

Relay Trust demonstrates one small part of that future: a receiving agent explains its decision from signed evidence and delegated capabilities, while refusing copied identities, altered claims, and withdrawn authority. Its synthetic laboratory exposes a deeper requirement for production: distribute custody and authenticate owners without making verification opaque.

The winning infrastructure will make trust portable, inspectable, and reversible. Agents should carry enough evidence for a counterparty to make a bounded decision, while owners retain the ability to stop future actions.
